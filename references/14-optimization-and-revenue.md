# Optimization and revenue

How AdSense earnings work after approval, and how to improve them
without breaking policy. No earning figure is promised here.
Third-party RPM benchmarks vary wildly by niche, country, season,
and traffic quality, and are not quoted in this skill.

## How earnings are counted now

- Since early 2024, AdSense for Content pays per impression
  (eCPM basis), not per click alone. Clicks still matter because
  advertiser demand follows performance, but the unit of payment
  is the impression.
- Publishers receive 80 percent of revenue after the buy-side fee
  for AdSense for Content, and about 68 percent overall when the
  buyer is Google Ads. See `06-terms-and-payments.md`.
- AdSense for Search keeps a 51 percent publisher share. See
  `17-youtube-and-other-products.md`.

## Metrics that matter

- Page RPM: estimated earnings per 1,000 page views. The headline
  number for a page or site.
- Impression RPM: estimated earnings per 1,000 ad impressions.
  Useful when comparing units with different fill.
- CTR: clicks divided by impressions. A diagnostic, not a goal to
  inflate. Never try to raise CTR by encouraging clicks.
- Active View viewable: the share of impressions that were
  viewable. Higher viewability attracts stronger bids.
- Fill rate: the share of ad requests that returned an ad. Low fill
  after heavy blocking usually means you removed too much demand.
- Earnings: the only number that pays. A higher RPM with far fewer
  impressions can mean less money. Judge changes by total earnings
  and user experience together.

## What actually moves earnings

1. Traffic volume and quality. Real, engaged readers in
   advertiser-rich countries matter more than any setting. This is
   the largest lever by far.
2. Viewability. Ads that load where readers actually look, on fast
   pages, earn more than ads buried below slow content.
3. Page speed. Slow pages lose impressions before ads load. Fix
   speed before adding more units.
4. Content depth and intent. Pages that fully answer a question
   keep readers longer and attract better demand than thin pages.
5. Layout testing. Small measured changes in placement and format
   compound over time.
6. Seasonality. Advertiser demand rises and falls through the year,
   with the year-end shopping season usually strongest. Compare a
   change against the same period, not against last month alone.

## AdSense experiments

Experiments run a control and a variation at the same time to
different visitors, so market noise does not fool you.

- Change one major variable at a time: one placement, one format,
  or one setting.
- Let the experiment run long enough to cover weekday and weekend
  traffic. Community practice is at least 7 days and often 14.
  Google shows when an experiment has enough data. Follow the
  account's own signal.
- Read the result in total earnings and user experience, not one
  slot's RPM.
- Keep a rollback path. Note the old setting before you change it.
- Do not run overlapping experiments on the same pages. Results
  will be unreadable.

## Blocking controls and the Ad Review Center

Blocking controls live under Brand safety in the account. They are
brand-safety tools, not earnings levers.

- Every block removes bidders from your auctions. Fewer bidders
  usually means lower winning bids across the whole site.
- Use the Ad Review Center to block individual problem creatives.
  It is the most precise control and the right first step for a
  misleading or inappropriate ad.
- Use category or advertiser blocks only with a concrete reason:
  a legal requirement, a clear brand-safety issue, or a user-trust
  problem that outweighs the revenue loss.
- Do not block whole categories because their RPM looks low. Low
  paying demand still fills impressions that would otherwise go
  empty, and removing it often lowers total earnings.
- Review blocks regularly. A block that made sense once may be
  costing money now.

## Reports worth reading weekly

- By page: which pages earn, which get traffic but no earnings,
  and which have low viewability.
- By country: where your valuable traffic comes from, so content
  decisions follow real readers.
- By device: mobile versus desktop behaviour, since formats and
  viewability differ sharply.
- By ad unit: which placements perform, after accounting for
  traffic differences between pages.
- By ad type and platform: how Auto ads compare with manual units
  on the same pages.

Link AdSense to Google Analytics for a fuller picture of behaviour,
but keep the account's own reports as the earnings source of truth.

## Optimization tab

The Optimization tab shows tailored opportunities for the account,
such as format suggestions or Auto ads settings. Treat each
suggestion as a test candidate, not an order. Preview the change,
run it as an experiment where possible, and roll back if readers
or earnings suffer.

## What not to do for revenue

- Never buy traffic, exchange clicks, or use bots. See
  `05-program-policies-ads-traffic.md`.
- Never add units until the page is mostly ads. Short-term gains
  cost readers and risk policy action.
- Never copy another site's layout blindly. Their traffic and
  niche are different. Test on your own pages.
- Never trust a course that promises a specific RPM. Demand,
  geography, and season decide it, and no one outside Google can
  guarantee it.
