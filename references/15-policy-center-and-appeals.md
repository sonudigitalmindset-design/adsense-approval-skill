# Policy Center and appeals

How enforcement works after approval, how to read the Policy
Center, and how to appeal. Nothing here promises reinstatement.
Follow the exact wording of the notice in the account, since it
names the level, the pages, and the deadline that apply to that
case.

## Levels of enforcement

- Page level: ad serving is restricted or disabled on specific
  URLs. The rest of the site keeps serving. Fix the named pages.
- Site level: ad serving is restricted or disabled across a whole
  site. Fix the site-wide cause, not one page.
- Account level: ad serving stops across the account. This is the
  most serious state and needs a full review of every site and
  traffic source.
- Ad serving limited: a temporary state, often tied to invalid
  traffic concerns, where Google limits how many ads serve while
  it assesses traffic quality. It is a warning to clean up traffic
  sources, not a state to wait out passively.
- Account disabled or terminated: the account is closed for policy
  reasons. Payment handling follows the Terms. See
  `06-terms-and-payments.md`.

## Reading a Policy Center notice

Every notice should answer four questions. Find all four before
acting.

1. What level is it: page, site, or account?
2. Which URLs or products are named?
3. Which policy is cited, in Google's own words?
4. What is the deadline, and is a review request offered?

Save a copy of the notice with the date. Screenshots and emails
are the record if support later asks what changed and when.

## The fix workflow

1. Read the notice fully. Do not guess the cause from the title.
2. Open each named URL and find the violation yourself.
3. Fix the root cause. Remove violating content or files. Removing
   only the ads from a violating page is not a fix.
4. Check the rest of the site for the same issue. Enforcement
   often names examples, not every instance.
5. Wait until the fix is live and verified in your own browser.
6. Request a review from the Policy Center where the option is
   offered. Describe what changed, with URLs and dates.
7. Do not request review with no changes. Empty review requests
   waste the chance and can harden the decision.

## Invalid traffic enforcement

Google defines invalid traffic as clicks or impressions that do
not come from a real user with genuine interest. This includes
accidental clicks, automated traffic, and clicks encouraged by the
publisher. See `05-program-policies-ads-traffic.md` for the full
definition and prevention tips.

When ad serving is limited for invalid traffic concerns:

- Check traffic sources for spikes, unknown referrers, and
  suspicious patterns in the same period.
- Stop any paid traffic, traffic exchanges, and social campaigns
  aimed at ad-heavy pages until the cause is clear.
- Check placements for accidental-click risks: ads near buttons,
  floating ads over content, and misleading headings.
- Keep a written record of what you found and changed. It becomes
  the evidence for any appeal.

## The invalid traffic appeal form

Accounts disabled by the Ad Traffic Quality team are appealed
through one online form, linked from the AdSense Help page on
accounts disabled for invalid traffic. The form asks for:

- Your name and contact email for the disabled account.
- Your publisher ID in pub- form.
- Whether you or your site, app, or channel ever violated AdSense
  or Ad Manager policies or Terms, and how.
- The detailed reasons you believe explain the invalid activity.
- The changes you will implement to improve traffic quality.
- Traffic log or report data showing suspicious IPs, referrers, or
  requests that could explain the activity.

Rules for a credible appeal:

- Tell the truth completely. Do not deny a violation that happened.
  State what happened, when you found it, and what you changed.
- Give numbers and dates: when the spike started, which referrers
  appeared, what you blocked, and when.
- Attach or paste real evidence from logs and reports, not
  opinions about intent.
- Write your own facts. Copied appeal templates read as generic
  and help no one. See `examples/policy-appeal-outline.md` for a
  structure to fill in with your own facts.
- Submit once, carefully. Google says further appeals may not be
  considered after a decision, and a refused appeal means you may
  not open a new account.

## Waiting periods and support instructions

- Some support replies instruct a waiting period before another
  appeal or form submission. Follow the exact instruction in your
  own notice or email, including its length. Do not treat a waiting
  period from someone else's case as a general rule.
- Do not open a second account while an appeal is pending. A
  duplicate account for the same payee creates a new violation.
- Do not pay anyone who promises to restore an account. No third
  party can force a Google decision.

## After a fix or a successful appeal

- Keep the fix in place. Reverting content after review invites
  re-enforcement.
- Watch the Policy Center weekly and traffic sources daily for a
  while after any enforcement.
- Document the incident: notice, cause, fix, dates, and outcome.
  The record protects the next review.

## Where to get help

- Policy Center in the AdSense account: the notices, deadlines,
  and review buttons.
- AdSense Help Center: the appeal form link and the invalid
  traffic help pages.
- AdSense Help Community: peer help for interpretation, not
  decisions. Treat forum answers as community practice.
- Support contact options inside the account vary by account.
  There is no verified public AdSense phone number published by
  Google. Do not trust numbers from third-party sites.
