# Content quality guide

This file turns the approval rules into writing and publishing habits.
Google does not publish a word count or post count. It judges whether
the site genuinely helps readers.

## The one question

"Does this page genuinely help a real reader, in a way they could not
get in a few seconds elsewhere?" If the honest answer is no, the page
is not ready.

## Original value: what counts

- Your own tested experience: what you tried, what happened, what you
  measured, what went wrong.
- Your own comparisons, checklists, templates, screenshots, and worked
  examples.
- Clear steps written from doing the task, not restated from another
  article.
- Honest limits: what you did not test, what varies, and when to get
  professional help.
- Local detail that only someone in the place or situation would know.

## What does not count as original

- Rewriting another article with synonyms swapped.
- Summarising three articles into one without adding anything.
- Publishing raw AI output without editing, checking, or adding
  experience.
- Copying a government notice, job posting, or product description
  with no added explanation.
- Padding a short answer with filler until it looks long.

## AI-assisted content: the honest rule

- AI-assisted content is not banned as a category. Unedited mass AI
  production is a common rejection cause, because it usually produces
  generic text with no original insight.
- Use AI as a drafting aid, then edit hard: check every fact, remove
  invented numbers and fake quotes, add your own examples, and rewrite
  in your own voice.
- Never publish a claim, statistic, price, date, or quotation you have
  not verified from a primary source.
- Never present an illustrative story as a lived experience. If you
  did not do it, say so, or leave it out.
- Disclose AI-generated media where it could mislead, especially
  images or video about real people, places, or events.

## Copyright safety

- Write your own text. Short quotations with credit are different from
  copying whole passages.
- Use images you own, images with a licence that allows the use, or
  images from a reputable free library. Keep a note of the source and
  licence for each image.
- Do not describe or encourage copying a full textbook, a friend's
  full register, or another creator's whole work. Scanning and sharing
  copyrighted material is a copyright risk even when framed as a
  study tip.
- Do not embed or link to pirated movies, music, software, or books,
  and do not host downloads you do not have rights to share.
- If a rights holder complains, remove the material promptly and fix
  the process that let it through.

## YMYL topics: health, finance, legal

"Your money or your life" topics affect health, money, safety, or
legal rights. Reviewers weigh them hardest.

- Cite credible sources: official guidelines, peer-reviewed research,
  government pages, and established institutions. Link to the source.
- Give numbers with their source and date. A health or finance figure
  without a source is a trust failure.
- Qualify advice: state who it does and does not apply to, and when
  professional advice is needed.
- Never promise a cure, a guaranteed return, or a legal outcome.
- Show who wrote the page and why they are qualified to write it, or
  be honest that it is general information from research, not
  professional advice.
- Keep medical and financial pages current. An outdated dosage, tax
  rate, or scheme rule is worse than no page at all.

## Trust signals that reviewers notice

- A real author name and bio on every post, consistent across the site.
- An About page that names a real person or team and a real story.
- A working contact route that is actually monitored.
- Consistent dates: published and updated dates that are real, never
  backdated to fake age. Backdating misleads reviewers and can get a
  site flagged.
- Sources linked inline, not only listed at the end.
- Corrections handled openly: fix errors, update the date, and say
  what changed where it matters.
- No fake comments, fake reviews, fake follower counts, or fake
  testimonials. Fabricated engagement misleads visitors and can hurt
  review. Discussion comments posted openly under the author's own
  name are honest; comments impersonating readers are not.

## Structure that helps readers and reviewers

- One clear topic per post, answered early, then explained in depth.
- Short paragraphs, descriptive headings, and steps in order.
- One H1 per page, matching the page title.
- Internal links that help the reader, not keyword-stuffed links.
- A sitemap that lists real pages, and categories that group real
  posts.
- Alt text on images that describes the image honestly.

## Before publishing any post

1. Does it answer the question completely?
2. Is every fact checked against a primary source?
3. Is there at least one thing here that is mine: a test, an example,
   a comparison, a screenshot, a template?
4. Would I be comfortable with a reviewer reading this page with ads
   on it?
5. Does it follow the policy lists in `03-publisher-policies.md` and
   `04-publisher-restrictions.md`?

If any answer is no, fix the post before publishing. Publishing first
and fixing later leaves weak pages in the review.
