# Rejection reasons and fixes

Google shows the reason on the Sites page in AdSense and in the
decision email. The wording varies slightly, and the reason names a
category, not the specific pages that caused it. Always start from the
exact wording and date shown in the account.

## Low value content

**What it means:** Google believes the site does not offer enough
original, useful content to justify showing ads. It is a judgement
about the whole site, not a technical fault and not a ban. It is the
most reported rejection reason.

**Common patterns behind it:**

- Thin pages that barely answer the question.
- Duplicate, scraped, or lightly rewritten content with nothing added.
- Raw auto-generated or AI text published without editing, checking,
  or original insight.
- Pages built only for search ranking or only to hold ads.
- Tool, category, or tag pages with almost no text.
- Empty sections, demo content, "coming soon" pages, and broken menus.
- Affiliate-heavy pages with no original review, test, or comparison.
- Embeds (video, social posts, maps) with no original text around them.

**Fix plan:**

1. List every indexable URL from the sitemap or Search Console.
2. Mark each URL: keep, improve, merge, or delete.
3. Delete or noindex empty tag pages, test pages, and copied posts.
4. Rewrite kept pages so each one fully answers its question, with
   original examples, steps, screenshots, or tested experience.
5. Add real text to tool and category pages: what the tool does, how
   to use it, and answers to common questions.
6. Remove leftover demo content from the theme.
7. Put the main answer early in each post, then go deep. Length alone
   does not fix this: a long post full of filler is still low value.
8. Verify the fixes are live and check indexing before reapplying.

## Site behaviour: navigation problems

**What it means:** reviewers or users cannot move through the site
easily, or the site looks unfinished.

**Fix:** working menus on mobile and desktop, no dead links, no pages
under construction, a clear homepage that explains the site, and
categories that contain real posts. Click through the whole site as a
stranger would.

## Policy violation

**What it means:** some content on the site breaks the Publisher
Policies or Program policies. The reason may name a category.

**Fix:** audit the whole site against `03-publisher-policies.md` and
`04-publisher-restrictions.md`, including comments, downloads, embeds,
and outbound links. Remove or rewrite the violating content itself.
Removing ads from the page is not enough. If copyrighted material is
involved, remove the page or the file.

## Site down or unavailable

**What it means:** Google's crawler could not open the site, or the
site was not public during review.

**Fix:** make the site public, fix hosting and DNS problems, remove
password protection and "maintenance mode", check that robots.txt does
not block Googlebot, and confirm the site loads over HTTPS without
errors. Then request review again.

## Unsupported language

**What it means:** the site's primary language is not supported for
AdSense, or the language cannot be determined.

**Fix:** check the supported-language list on the live Help pages. If
the site mixes languages, make the primary language clear and
consistent, and make sure each page's language matches its content.

## Duplicate account

**What it means:** an AdSense account already exists for the same
payee, or the application matches an existing account.

**Fix:** sign in to the existing account instead of creating a new
one. Do not create a second account to work around a rejection. If the
existing account is inaccessible, follow the account recovery route in
the Help Center.

## Underage applicant

**What it means:** the applicant is under 18.

**Fix:** a parent or guardian signs up using their own Google Account.
Payments go to them. When the creator turns 18 and the account is still
open, a new account is not needed for that reason alone.

## Invalid traffic concerns at application stage

**What it means:** traffic sources look artificial, incentivised, or
bought, or the site is promoted in prohibited ways.

**Fix:** stop all paid-to-click, exchange, autosurf, and bot traffic.
Stop asking anyone to click ads. Remove misleading promotion. Build
genuine traffic from search, social sharing, and email before
reapplying.

## Reapply discipline

1. Read the reason and date. Note them down.
2. Fix every weak page, not just a few.
3. Verify the fixes live in your own browser.
4. Wait until the fixed pages are indexed where that matters.
5. Request review once, from the same account, on the same site.
6. While waiting, keep improving the site. Do not remove and re-add
   the site, and do not open a second account.
7. If rejected again, repeat the audit from scratch. A second
   rejection with the same reason usually means the fix did not go far
   enough, or a whole category of pages was missed.

## What not to do after a rejection

- Do not reapply the same day with no changes.
- Do not add ten more thin posts and call it fixed.
- Do not buy traffic, backlinks, or "approval services" that promise
  a pass.
- Do not hide violating content from the reviewer while keeping it
  live for visitors.
- Do not pay anyone who claims to work inside Google's review team.
