# Glossary: AdSense terms in plain language

Each term has one plain definition and one example. Teach the term,
then the example, then move on.

- **AdSense:** Google's program that lets website, app, and video
  publishers show Google ads and earn a share of the revenue.
  Example: a blog shows display ads and earns money per thousand ad
  impressions.

- **AdSense for Content:** AdSense ads on website pages. Since early
  2024, publishers are paid per impression (eCPM) for this inventory.

- **AdSense for Search:** AdSense ads on a site's own search results
  pages. Its revenue share stays at 51% and it was not part of the
  2024 per-impression change.

- **AdSense for YouTube:** the AdSense account type linked to a
  YouTube channel through the YouTube Partner Programme. Website
  approval and YouTube approval are separate paths.

- **ads.txt:** a public text file at the root of a domain listing the
  companies authorised to sell the site's ad inventory. Example:
  `example.com/ads.txt` contains a line with the publisher ID and the
  word DIRECT.

- **Auto ads:** a single AdSense code snippet that lets Google decide
  where to place ads on the page. Launched in February 2018.

- **Better Ads Standards:** industry standards for ad experiences that
  users find acceptable. Intrusive formats fail them.

- **Click-through rate (CTR):** clicks divided by impressions, shown
  as a percentage. A sudden unexplained CTR spike is an invalid
  traffic warning sign.

- **CMP (consent management platform):** a tool that asks visitors for
  consent to cookies and data use and records their choice. For EEA,
  UK, and Switzerland traffic, Google requires a Google-certified CMP.

- **CPC (cost per click):** the old main payment model, where the
  publisher was paid when a user clicked an ad. Replaced for AdSense
  for Content by per-impression payment in 2024.

- **CPM / eCPM:** cost per thousand impressions. eCPM is the effective
  CPM actually earned. Since 2024, AdSense for Content pays on an
  eCPM basis.

- **DART cookie:** the former name for the DoubleClick cookie used to
  serve ads based on visits to a site. Privacy policies historically
  disclosed it. Modern disclosures should name cookies and identifiers
  used for advertising in current terms.

- **DMCA:** the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act. Google responds
  to DMCA notices of alleged copyright infringement.

- **Invalid traffic (IVT):** clicks or impressions that do not come
  from genuine user interest, including bot traffic, self-clicks,
  incentivised clicks, and click exchanges. Earnings from invalid
  traffic are deducted.

- **Low value content:** Google's rejection reason for a site that
  does not offer enough original, useful content to justify ads. A
  whole-site judgement, not one bad post.

- **MFA (made for AdSense):** thin sites built only to carry ads, with
  no original content. Policy enforcement from 2007 to 2008 and the
  2011 Panda update largely ended this model.

- **Payee:** the person or business that owns the AdSense account and
  receives payment. One AdSense account per payee.

- **Payment threshold:** the minimum balance that must be reached
  before a payment is issued. US $100 or the local equivalent. Confirm
  the current figure on the live Help pages.

- **PIN verification:** address verification by a personal
  identification number mailed by post. Enter it in the account inside
  the deadline shown.

- **Policy Center:** the section of the AdSense account that shows
  policy issues on pages, sites, and the account, with deadlines for
  fixes.

- **Program policies:** the AdSense-specific rules on clicks,
  impressions, traffic sources, ad behaviour, and privacy, in addition
  to the Google Publisher Policies.

- **Publisher ID:** the account identifier in the form
  `pub-XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX` (16 digits). It appears in the ad code and
  the ads.txt line. Treat it as account data: do not share it in
  public screenshots.

- **Publisher Policies:** Google's cross-product rules on what content
  and behaviour cannot be monetised. Prohibited content gets no ads.

- **Publisher Restrictions:** Google's list of content categories that
  may carry ads but receive less advertising demand. Restricted is not
  the same as prohibited.

- **Revenue share:** the split of advertising revenue between Google
  (and the buying platform) and the publisher. Since 2024, AdSense for
  Content publishers receive 80% after the advertiser platform's fee.

- **RPM (revenue per thousand impressions):** earnings per 1,000 ad
  impressions. Page RPM is earnings per 1,000 page views. Since the
  2024 change, RPM is the number to track.

- **Site review:** Google's review of an entire site against the
  Program policies before ads serve. Usually a few days, sometimes 2
  to 4 weeks.

- **YMYL (your money or your life):** topics that can affect health,
  money, safety, or legal rights. Held to a higher standard for
  expertise, sourcing, and accuracy.
