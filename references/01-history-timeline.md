# AdSense history timeline: 2003 to today

> How to use this file: dates marked **confirmed** come from Google's own
> announcement or Help pages as reported in the sources listed in
> `11-official-sources.md`. Dates marked **reported** come from reputable
> trade press. Anything else is labelled approximate. Policies change, so
> recheck the live official pages before quoting a current rule.

## 2003: launch

- **March 2003 (confirmed):** Google announces content-targeting
  advertising services for publishers, the precursor to AdSense.
- **April 2003 (confirmed):** Google acquires Applied Semantics. The
  AdSense name came from Applied Semantics' competing product.
- **18 June 2003 (confirmed):** Google launches the self-service AdSense
  program from Mountain View, California. Publishers apply online, Google
  reviews each application against editorial guidelines, and approved
  publishers serve text ads from AdWords advertisers and earn a share of
  clicks. At launch the program is for English-language websites
  worldwide. A premium tier exists for publishers with more than 20
  million page views per month.
- **What mattered then:** contextual text ads, no pop-ups, relevance to
  the page. The same idea still sits at the centre of approval today:
  ads must match real content on a real page.

## 2004 to 2006: growth and the first crackdowns

- **2004 (confirmed):** Some advertisers complain AdSense traffic
  converts worse than search traffic. Google lets advertisers opt out of
  the AdSense network.
- **May 2005 (reported):** AdSense for Feeds starts as a limited
  participation beta. It opens to all AdSense users in August 2008 and
  is later retired.
- **Early 2005 (reported):** AdSense accounts for an estimated 15% of
  Google's total revenue.
- **2005 to 2008 (reported):** "Made for AdSense" (MFA) sites boom:
  thin pages built only to carry ads. Click fraud becomes a serious
  problem, with contemporary estimates putting it at 10 to 15% of clicks
  at the peak.

## 2007 to 2011: quality becomes the rule

- **2007 to 2008 (reported):** Google updates AdSense policies to
  require "substantial and unique content" and "real value" to users.
  Sites that exist purely to show ads, with no original content, are
  banned. This is the origin of today's "low value content" rejection.
- **October 2008 (confirmed):** Google discontinues the cost-per-action
  beta in favour of a DoubleClick offering.
- **February 2010 (reported):** AdSense starts using search history in
  contextual matching to offer more relevant ads.
- **2011 (confirmed):** The Google Panda search update demotes thin and
  low-quality content in search results. Combined with AdSense policy
  enforcement, it effectively ends the MFA era. Lesson: approval and
  search quality moved together from here on.

## 2012 to 2016: formats, limits, and rebranding

- **2012 (reported):** A grey arrow appears under AdSense text ads to
  make them easier to identify.
- **21 January 2014 (reported):** Google launches Direct Campaigns, a
  tool for publishers to sell ads directly. It is retired on
  10 February 2015.
- **18 June 2015 (confirmed):** Google announces an AdSense rebrand with
  a new logo.
- **2016 (reported):** Google removes the three-ads-per-page limit.
  The rule becomes a balance test instead: advertising and promotional
  material should not exceed page content. Do not quote the old
  three-ad limit as a current rule.

## 2018 to 2020: automation and site-level review

- **21 February 2018 (reported):** Google launches Auto ads, replacing
  page-level ads. One code snippet lets Google decide placement.
- **2018 to 2019 (approximate):** Site approval tightens. New sites
  must pass a review of the whole site before ads serve, and each
  additional site needs its own approval. The "Sites" page in AdSense
  becomes the place where approval status and reasons are shown.
- **2020 onwards (reported):** Review becomes largely automated, with
  human review for edge cases and appeals. The editorial team review
  described in the 2003 announcement is no longer how most applications
  are decided.

## 2023 to 2024: the payment model changes

- **November 2023 (confirmed):** Google announces two changes for
  AdSense for Content: payment moves from mostly cost-per-click to
  per-impression (eCPM), and the revenue share is restructured into
  separate buy-side and sell-side rates.
- **12 February 2024 (confirmed):** Google confirms the change has
  rolled out. Publishers receive 80% of revenue after the advertiser
  platform takes its fee. When the buyer is Google Ads, whose average
  buy-side fee is about 15%, the overall publisher share is expected to
  remain around 68%. AdSense for Search is not affected and stays at
  51%.
- **April 2024 (reported):** Ad Intents launches, a format that links
  page text to search ads. It expands to display advertising options in
  May 2025.
- **2024 (reported):** Offerwall messages enter beta. Offerwall
  launches for Google Ad Manager on 26 June 2025 and becomes generally
  available on AdSense in April 2026 (reported).

## 2025 to today: what to verify live

- Google is migrating and consolidating the Google Publisher Policies
  and Google Publisher Restrictions into a new Publisher Policies Help
  Center. The same policies remain viewable in the AdMob, AdSense, and
  Ad Manager Help Centers during the migration.
- AdSense turns 23 on 18 June 2026. The program still rests on the 2003
  bargain: original content that serves readers, genuine user interest
  behind every click and impression, and honest disclosure about data
  use.
- **June 2026, identity verification before ads (OFFICIAL):** from
  June 2026, publishers may need to verify their identity before
  the account can start showing ads, by submitting a
  government-issued photo ID and, depending on location, taking
  a video selfie. The window is 45 days from the first request,
  after which Google stops showing ads or pauses activation.
  Verified on the live AdSense Help page "Verify your identity
  using online verification" on 17 November 2026. The account
  setup page describes the same step from June 2026. Full
  process details are in
  `references/16-verification-and-payments-troubleshooting.md`.

## The through-line for learners

1. 2003 to 2006: anyone with a site could join, and abuse followed.
2. 2007 to 2011: quality rules arrived and thin sites were pushed out.
3. 2012 to 2019: formats automated, placement rules became a balance
   test, and whole-site review became the gate.
4. 2023 to today: payment is per impression, policy enforcement is
   mostly automated, and the publisher is responsible for content,
   traffic, placement, and privacy disclosure together.
