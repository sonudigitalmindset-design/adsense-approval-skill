# Staying updated

AdSense rules, Help pages, and account features change.
Google says keeping up to date is the publisher's
responsibility. This guide teaches the habit: which
channels to watch, how often, and how to tell an official
change from a rumour.

[Back to the main page](../README.md)

## The official channels

Check these first. When any other source disagrees with
them, these win.

- AdSense Help Center: https://support.google.com/adsense/
  The source of truth for eligibility, site review,
  policies, payments, and troubleshooting. Search it by
  exact topic and read the page, not a summary of it.
- AdSense Help Community: linked from the Help Center.
  Other publishers answer questions here. Treat answers
  as discussion, not authority, unless a Google
  representative confirms the point. Use it to find
  which Help page answers a question.
- The AdSense account itself: the home page, the Sites
  page, the Policy Center, and the Payments page show
  the deadlines and actions that apply to a specific
  account. Account notices beat every guide, including
  this skill.
- Email from Google: policy updates, payment notices,
  and verification requests arrive by email to the
  account address. Read them in full. Do not act on a
  forwarded screenshot of an email. Open the account and
  confirm the same notice is there.
- Inside AdSense blog: https://adsense.googleblog.com/
  Google's publisher blog. Check the date on every post.
  Old posts may describe features or rules that no
  longer exist. The Help Center wins over any blog post.
- Search Central Blog and Search Central documentation:
  https://developers.google.com/search/blog and
  https://developers.google.com/search/docs/
  For Search-side guidance that affects review
  readiness: helpful content, spam policies, page
  experience, and structured data. These are Search
  rules, not AdSense rules. Keep the boundary clear.
- Google Publisher Policies Help Center: linked from the
  Publisher Policies page. Google is migrating policies
  there from the older Help locations. Follow the live
  redirect when a bookmarked page moves.

## The monthly 10 minute check

Once a month, do this short pass:

1. Open the AdSense account home page. Read every notice
   and open action.
2. Open the Policy Center. Note any new flag or deadline.
3. Open the Help Center and search for one topic that
   matters to the site right now, such as payments,
   verification, or a policy category. Skim the page for
   wording that differs from what the skill says.
4. If a page contradicts this skill, note the date, the
   page URL, and the exact wording. Update the skill
   following [Contributing](contributing.md).

This habit catches most changes while they are still
small. It costs ten minutes and prevents months of work
built on an outdated rule.

## How to verify a claimed change

A claim about a new rule arrives from a video, a forum
post, or a screenshot. Before acting on it:

1. Ask for the Help page link. A real change has a Help
   page, an account notice, or an official blog post.
2. Open the link. Read the page. Note the date it was
   read.
3. Check whether the page actually says what the claim
   says. Quotes get shortened and dates get moved.
4. Check the account. If the change affects a specific
   account, the account usually shows it.
5. If no official page supports the claim, treat it as
   unverified. Do not rebuild a site around it.

Record what was checked: the claim, the page, the date,
and the verdict. A dated log beats a memory of having
read something somewhere.

## Rumour patterns to distrust

- Screenshots with no link. A screenshot can be old,
  cropped, or from another product. Ask for the live
  page.
- "A Google insider told me." Reviewers do not leak
  checklists to courses and channels. Treat insider
  claims as marketing.
- "Google now requires X posts, Y words, or Z months."
  Google publishes no minimum post count, word count,
  traffic level, or universal domain age rule. Any
  number attached to approval is either a practice
  heuristic or invented.
- Old posts presented as news. A 2023 post about a
  policy change is history, not news. Check the date
  before sharing.
- Guarantees. "Approval in 7 days", "100 percent
  approval method", and "approved account for sale" are
  all red flags. See
  `references/07-rejection-reasons-and-fixes.md` for
  the honest path after a rejection.
- Panic headlines about mass bans. Enforcement waves
  happen, but a headline without a Help page link and
  without Policy Center evidence in the account is not
  a reason to change a healthy site.

## What to do when a real change lands

1. Read the official page in full, not the headline.
2. Decide whether it affects the site. Many changes
   affect only one product, one region, or one format.
3. Change the site once, in one place, and verify live.
4. Record the change, the date, and the URLs touched.
5. Update the skill if it teaches the old rule, and
   record the change in `CHANGELOG.md`.

## Keeping this skill current

This skill records the date each official claim was
verified in `references/11-official-sources.md`. When a
recheck finds a different wording, update the reference,
the source list, and the changelog together. A skill
that quietly goes stale is worse than no skill, because
it speaks with confidence about rules that moved.
