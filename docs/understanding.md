# Understanding AdSense approval

How Google AdSense approval actually works, in plain language.
No myths, no invented minimums, no guaranteed outcomes.

[Back to the main page](../README.md)

## The short version

AdSense pays you for showing Google ads on your site. Before
the first ad can show, Google reviews the whole site. The
review asks three questions: does the site follow the
policies, is the content original and useful, and is the
applicant a real person who controls the site. A reviewer
answers those questions by reading the site, not by counting
posts or measuring traffic.

## The three policy layers

Google's rules sit in three layers. Confusing them causes
most misunderstandings.

- **Publisher Policies.** Prohibited content and behaviour.
  Break these and the site cannot carry ads at all. Read
  [`references/03-publisher-policies.md`](../references/03-publisher-policies.md).
- **Publisher Restrictions.** Restricted content that can
  carry ads but gets less demand and less revenue. Read
  [`references/04-publisher-restrictions.md`](../references/04-publisher-restrictions.md).
- **Program Policies.** The rules about ads, traffic, and
  behaviour: invalid traffic, click encouragement, traffic
  sources, placement, and privacy. These are the rules that
  close accounts. Read
  [`references/05-program-policies-ads-traffic.md`](../references/05-program-policies-ads-traffic.md).

## What Google officially requires

Four eligibility checks, from the official Help pages:

1. You are 18 or older, or you apply through a parent or
   guardian.
2. You own or control the site and can edit its HTML.
3. The site carries original, high-quality content.
4. The site complies with the Program policies.

That is the full official list. Google publishes **no**
minimum post count, **no** minimum word count, **no**
minimum traffic level, and **no** universal domain age rule.
Any checklist that states one as a Google rule is inventing
it. Read
[`references/02-eligibility-and-application.md`](../references/02-eligibility-and-application.md).

## What the review looks at

The review covers the whole site, not one page. One
violating page can hold back an otherwise clean site. A
review usually takes a few days and sometimes takes two to
four weeks. The reviewer weighs content value and
originality, trust signals, navigation and completeness,
and policy fit in context. The decision is human judgement
on the whole site.

What a script can check, what only a human can judge, and
what no outsider can see at all is laid out in
[`references/31-approval-check-matrix.md`](../references/31-approval-check-matrix.md).
The signals a reviewer may weigh without Google ever
publishing them as rules are collected, each labelled as
inferred, in
[`references/33-unpublished-review-signals.md`](../references/33-unpublished-review-signals.md).

## The myths that waste the most time

- "You need 30 posts." No published minimum exists.
- "You need 10,000 visitors a month." No published
  traffic minimum exists.
- "Your domain must be 6 months old." No universal age
  rule exists.
- "AI content is banned." Google judges helpfulness and
  originality, not the tool used. Copied or low-value
  content fails whatever wrote it. Read
  [`references/09-content-quality-guide.md`](../references/09-content-quality-guide.md).
- "A script can guarantee approval." No script can match
  the decision. A script checks the visible surface. The
  decision stays with Google.

## The money, briefly

AdSense for Content moved to per-impression payment in
early 2024, paying 80 percent after the buy-side fee, about
68 percent overall when the buyer is Google Ads. AdSense
for Search stayed at 51 percent. The payment threshold is
US $100 or the local equivalent. The full history, with
confirmed dates separated from reported ones, is in
[`references/01-history-timeline.md`](../references/01-history-timeline.md),
and the terms and payment detail is in
[`references/06-terms-and-payments.md`](../references/06-terms-and-payments.md).
