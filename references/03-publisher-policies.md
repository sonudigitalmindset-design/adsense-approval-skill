# Google Publisher Policies: prohibited content and behaviour

Source of truth: Google Publisher Policies
(`support.google.com/admob/answer/10502938`) and the AdSense Program
policies (`support.google.com/adsense/answer/48182`). Google is
migrating these policies to a new Publisher Policies Help Center.
Failure to comply may result in Google blocking ads against the
content, or suspending or terminating the account.

Rule of thumb: prohibited content gets **no ads**. If a page matches
any item below, do not place AdSense code on it, and do not try to keep
the content while hiding it from review. Remove or rewrite the content.

These policies apply to the whole monetised property, including
user-generated content, comments, embedded media, downloads, outbound
links, and pages that are not central to the site.

## Content policies

### Illegal content

- Content that is illegal, promotes illegal activity, or infringes on
  the legal rights of others.
- Examples: instructions or facilitation for wrongdoing, content that
  violates applicable law in the place it is served.

### Intellectual property abuse

- Content that infringes copyright. Google responds to DMCA notices of
  alleged infringement.
- Selling or promoting counterfeit products: goods carrying a trademark
  or logo identical to, or substantially indistinguishable from, another
  brand's trademark.
- Practical checks: no copied text, no images taken from search results
  without rights, no pirated movies, music, software, or books, no
  unauthorised streaming links, no fake brand claims.

### Dangerous or derogatory content

- Content that incites hatred against, promotes discrimination of, or
  disparages a person or group on the basis of race or ethnic origin,
  religion, disability, age, nationality, veteran status, sexual
  orientation, gender, gender identity, or another characteristic
  associated with systemic discrimination or marginalisation.
- Examples: promoting hate groups or hate group paraphernalia,
  encouraging others to believe a person or group is inhuman, inferior,
  or worthy of being hated.
- Also covered: harassment, threats, content promoting self-harm,
  praise of violence, and support for terrorism or organised crime.
  Treat any signal here as an immediate hard blocker.

### Animal cruelty

- Content that promotes cruelty or gratuitous violence towards animals,
  for example promoting animal cruelty for entertainment such as cock
  or dog fighting.

### Misrepresentative content

- **Misleading representation:** content that misleads users about the
  publisher, the content, or its purpose.
- **Unreliable and harmful claims:** demonstrably false claims that
  undermine participation in elections or democratic processes, harmful
  health claims that contradict scientific consensus, and climate
  claims that contradict authoritative scientific consensus.
- **Deceptive practices:** scams, phishing, fake endorsements, and
  schemes designed to deceive users or extract money or data under
  false pretences.
- **Manipulated media:** media edited to deceive users about politics,
  social issues, or matters of public concern. Disclose AI-generated
  media where it could mislead.

### Enabling dishonest behaviour

- Content that enables dishonest behaviour, for example hacking
  instructions, software cracking, cheating services, fake documents,
  or academic dishonesty services.

### Sexually explicit content

- Publishers may not place Google ads on content that promotes sexually
  explicit content. Google's own test: if the content is something the
  publisher would not be comfortable viewing at work or with family
  around, it is probably not appropriate for Google ads.
- Child safety is absolute: no child endangerment, grooming,
  sextortion, sexualisation of minors, child trafficking, or
  child sexual abuse material in any form. Treat any signal as an
  immediate hard blocker and remove the content.

### Other prohibited content areas

- Content promoting paid sexual acts or cross-border marriage broker
  services, and adult themes placed in family content.
- Screens with no publisher content, low-value content, or
  under-construction content. Content must be valuable to the user and
  the focal point of the page. Do not place ads on thank-you pages,
  exit pages, error pages, or other dead-end screens.
- Replicated content: scraping or rewriting content from other sources
  without adding value, copying with light changes such as synonym
  swapping, and automatically generated content without manual review
  or curation.
- Content that requires sensitive-event restraint when it exploits,
  denies, or is insensitive towards an active crisis or unexpected
  event.

## Behavioural policies

- No invalid traffic and no encouragement of clicks. See
  `05-program-policies-ads-traffic.md` for the full click, traffic,
  and placement rules.
- No deceptive implementation: ads must not be placed where users will
  click them by accident, ads must not be disguised as content or
  navigation, and ads must not be embedded in software applications.
- No ad obstruction: ads covering content, content covering ads, hidden
  video ad controls, or unsupported video implementations.

## Privacy-related policies

- Publishers must disclose how data is collected and used, including
  cookies and identifiers used for advertising, and must obtain consent
  where the law requires it. See `05-program-policies-ads-traffic.md`.
- In the US and Canada, housing, employment, and credit-related ads
  cannot be targeted by gender, age, parental status, marital status,
  or ZIP/postal code.

## Requirements and other standards

- Sites must follow the technical and quality requirements Google
  publishes for the product in use, including landing page quality for
  paid traffic and the Better Ads Standards for ad experience.
- Keep the site crawlable, fast enough to use, and free of malware,
  unwanted software, pop-ups that block content, and redirects to
  unwanted sites.

## Audit habit

For every post, download, comment section, and embed, ask:

1. Did I create this, or do I have the rights to use it?
2. Could any part mislead, harm, or exploit a reader?
3. Would I be comfortable showing this page to a reviewer with ads on
   it?

If any answer is no, fix the page before applying. Removing the ads
from a violating page does not fix the violation for approval: the
content itself must go or change.
