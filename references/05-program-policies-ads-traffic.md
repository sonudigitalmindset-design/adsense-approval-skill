# AdSense Program policies: clicks, traffic, placement, privacy

Source of truth: AdSense Program policies
(`support.google.com/adsense/answer/48182`) and "Top invalid traffic
and policy violations that lead to account closure"
(`support.google.com/adsense/answer/2660562`). All publishers must
follow the Google Publisher Policies plus these Program policies.
Google reserves the right to disable ad serving to a site or disable
the account at any time for non-compliance.

## Invalid clicks and impressions

- Publishers may not click their own ads, for any reason. This includes
  testing, opening the advertiser's page, and supporting earnings.
- Clicks must result from genuine user interest. Any method that
  artificially generates clicks or impressions is strictly prohibited,
  including repeated manual clicks, automated click and impression
  tools, robots, and deceptive software.
- Do not test with live ads. Use the preview and test tools Google
  provides, or check placement without clicking.
- For YouTube creators: when viewing your own videos, skip the ad so
  advertiser costs are not artificially inflated.

## Encouraging clicks or views

Except for rewarded inventory, publishers may not ask others to click
or view ads, or use deceptive implementation to obtain clicks or views.

Prohibited examples:

- "Click the ads to support us", "support this site by clicking",
  or any request to click or refresh ads.
- Compensating users for viewing ads or performing searches, or
  promising compensation to a third party for such behaviour.
- Placing misleading labels near ads, disguising ads as content,
  menus, or download buttons, or placing ads where accidental clicks
  are likely.
- Offering rewards, points, content unlocks, or donations in exchange
  for ad clicks, outside a properly configured rewarded format.

Allowed: a neutral label such as "Advertisements" or "Sponsored links"
that honestly identifies the ad unit.

## Traffic sources

Google ads may not be placed on pages receiving traffic from certain
sources. Sites displaying Google ads may not:

- Use third-party services that generate clicks or impressions, such
  as paid-to-click, paid-to-surf, autosurf, and click-exchange
  programs.
- Be promoted through unsolicited mass emails or unwanted
  advertisements on third-party websites.
- Display ads as the result of software application actions, such as
  toolbars.
- Be loaded by software that triggers pop-ups, redirects users to
  unwanted websites, modifies browser settings, or interferes with
  site navigation.
- Receive traffic from online advertising unless the site complies
  with the spirit of Google's Landing Page Quality Guidelines.

Publisher responsibility: the publisher remains responsible for traffic
even when a third party, freelancer, advertiser, bot, or malicious
visitor caused the activity. Monitor sources, block problematic
referrers, and do not buy traffic sold as a way to earn ad revenue.

Acceptable traffic: organic search, direct visits, genuine social
shares, email to an opted-in list, and honest paid promotion that lands
on a compliant page.

## Ad placement and ad behaviour

- Ads must be clearly distinguishable from content. Do not style ad
  units to look like article text, navigation, or buttons.
- Advertising and promotional material should not exceed page content.
  The content must be the focal point of the page.
- Do not place ads on screens without publisher content, on
  under-construction pages, on error, thank-you, or exit pages, or on
  pages where the user's attention is clearly elsewhere.
- Do not place ads in pop-ups, pop-unders, or floating boxes that
  cover content. Do not use auto-refreshing pages to inflate
  impressions.
- Do not modify the AdSense code in ways Google does not allow, and do
  not place ads in emails, software applications, or unsupported
  placements.
- Keep ads away from download buttons, play buttons, and form submit
  buttons. Accidental-click layouts are a classic enforcement trigger.
- Follow the Better Ads Standards for ad experience. Intrusive formats
  harm both users and approval chances.

## Privacy, cookies, and consent

- Publishers must disclose, in a privacy policy, that third parties
  including Google may use cookies and identifiers to serve and
  measure ads, and must explain how users can manage ad personalisation
  and opt out.
- Where consent is required by law, obtain it before setting
  advertising cookies or collecting data for ads. For visitors in the
  EEA, the UK, and Switzerland, use a Google-certified consent
  management platform (CMP) and keep a working consent banner.
- Do not collect or send personal data to Google in ways the policies
  forbid, and do not use ad data to identify individual users.
- Keep the privacy disclosure accurate for every ad partner and
  analytics tool actually in use on the site, not only Google.

## Account closure: the most common causes

From Google's own list of common closure reasons:

Invalid traffic:

- Clicking ads on your own site, channel, or app.
- One or more users repeatedly clicking the ads.
- Asking others to click, offering rewards, or promising donations for
  clicks.
- Automated or bot traffic, and paid-to-click or click-exchange
  services.
- Manipulating ad targeting to get higher-revenue ads.

Policy violations:

- Sexually explicit content.
- Violations in user-generated content on pages carrying ad code.
- Copyright infringement and other prohibited content listed in
  `03-publisher-policies.md`.

The list is not exhaustive. Any violation can affect eligibility for
AdSense and other Google publisher products.

## Daily safety habits after approval

1. Check the Policy Center and fix flagged issues before deadlines.
2. Watch for sudden spikes in clicks, impressions, or click-through
   rate, and for strange referrers or geographies.
3. Never click your own ads, even once "to check".
4. Review new comments, uploads, and embeds before they go live on
   pages with ads.
5. Re-read the Program policies after any Google policy email. The
   publisher's job is to stay current.
