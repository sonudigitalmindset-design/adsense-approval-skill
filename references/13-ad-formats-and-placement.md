# Ad formats and placement

What each AdSense format is, when it fits, and the placement rules
that apply to all of them. Format names and settings change, so
confirm the live Ads page in the account before relying on a name
here. No format is promised to earn more than another.

## Two ways to show ads

- Auto ads: one code snippet on every page. Google chooses
  placements and formats. You control which formats are allowed,
  which pages and page areas are excluded, and some format options.
- Ad units: you create each unit and paste its code exactly where
  you want it. You control placement, and Google fills the unit.

Most publishers start with Auto ads, then add manual units for
placements they want to control. Neither method is officially
claimed to earn more or load faster than the other.

## Auto ads formats

- Anchor ads: a banner that sticks to the top or bottom edge of the
  screen, mainly on mobile. It must be easy to dismiss and must not
  cover navigation, cookie notices, or sticky menus.
- Vignette ads: a full-screen ad shown between page loads when the
  visitor follows a link. It is skippable. Keep frequency low and
  exclude links where an interstitial would annoy, such as login or
  checkout steps.
- Side rail ads: ads shown in the side margins on wide screens.
  They only appear where the layout has room.
- In-page ads: banner and native style ads placed inside the page
  content area by Auto ads. Watch how many appear before the first
  paragraph ends.
- Multiplex ads: a grid of recommended style ads, often at the end
  of content. They can also be created as manual units.
- Intent-driven formats: ad intents turn existing page text into
  links or chips, and related search shows search suggestions.
  These change page text, so preview them before enabling.

## Manual ad units

- Display ads: the standard responsive or fixed-size banner unit.
  Responsive units resize to the container and device. Fixed sizes
  restrict the inventory that can fill the unit.
- In-feed ads: ads that sit inside a list of posts or products and
  match the feed's card style. They must still be recognisable as
  ads and must not mimic content cards exactly.
- In-article ads: native style ads placed between paragraphs inside
  an article. They blend with text styling but must stay clearly
  labelled.
- Multiplex ads: a grid unit you place yourself, usually below
  content.
- Search engine unit: a Google powered search box for the site.
  Earnings and rules for AdSense for Search are separate. See
  `17-youtube-and-other-products.md`.

Older guides mention Matched content, a recommendation grid that
mixed your own posts with ads. It no longer appears in the current
format lists. Do not look for it or follow old setup steps for it.

## Placement rules that apply to every format

These come from the Program policies. Read the full wording in
`05-program-policies-ads-traffic.md`.

- Ads must not be placed under misleading headings or next to
  elements that make them look like content, navigation, or
  download controls.
- Ads must not sit so close to buttons, links, or menus that users
  click them by accident.
- Ads must not cover content, and content must not require
  dismissing an ad to be read.
- Ads must not appear on pages with no content value, such as
  error pages, blank pages, or pages whose only purpose is ads.
- Ads must not appear on pages with prohibited content. Remove the
  content, not just the ad.
- Do not modify the ad code. Do not hide units, stack units on top
  of each other, or place units in containers with no width.
- Sticky and floating ads are allowed only where they comply with
  the placement rules, including easy dismissal and no content
  obstruction.

## Placement practice that respects readers

- Keep content dominant. A page should read as an article with ads,
  not as ads with an article attached.
- Put the first ad after the reader has seen real content, not
  before the first line.
- In-article units work best after two or three paragraphs of
  genuine content, not in the middle of a list of links.
- Test one change at a time with AdSense experiments instead of
  moving everything at once. See
  `14-optimization-and-revenue.md`.
- Preview Auto ads on a real phone before turning on overlay
  formats. Check that menus, consent banners, and forms still work.
- Exclude pages where ads do not belong: login, signup, checkout,
  thank-you, error, and admin-facing pages.
- Keep the ad count per page modest on short pages. A short page
  with many units looks like it exists for ads.

## Common placement questions

- How many ads per page? Google removed the old three ads per page
  limit in 2016 (reported). There is no published current number
  limit. The rule is balance and user experience, not a count.
- Can ads go in the header? Yes, where they do not push content
  away or cause accidental clicks. Test on mobile.
- Can ads go in a sidebar? Yes. Sidebar units are standard, but a
  sidebar full of ads with thin main content still fails the value
  test.
- Can ads go inside a popup? No. Ads in popups and ads that appear
  through deceptive navigation are not allowed.
