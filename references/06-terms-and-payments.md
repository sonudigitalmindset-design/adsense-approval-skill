# Terms and payments: the contract behind approval

This file summarises the AdSense Terms and Conditions and the payment
system in plain language. It is general information, not legal advice.
The Terms in the publisher's AdSense account, in the publisher's
country version, control. Recheck the live Terms and Help pages before
advising on any payment, hold, or termination question.

## The contract in one paragraph

AdSense is a contract between the publisher and Google. The publisher
agrees to follow the Terms and Conditions, the Program policies, and
the Google Publisher Policies, to keep them current as they change, and
to accept that Google may disable ad serving or terminate the account
for violations. The publisher is paid a share of advertising revenue
under the revenue-share structure in force, subject to verification,
thresholds, and deductions for invalid traffic.

## Key terms every publisher should understand

- **Eligibility and one account:** the applicant must be 18 or over and
  may hold one AdSense account per payee. Details must be accurate and
  kept current.
- **Policy compliance is ongoing:** approval is not a one-time pass.
  Policies apply for the whole life of the account, and Google may
  change them at any time.
- **Invalid traffic:** earnings from invalid traffic are deducted.
  Repeated or serious invalid traffic can lead to suspension or
  termination, and in termination cases the balance may be forfeited
  under the Terms. The exact forfeiture wording depends on the Terms
  version and the reason for termination. Do not quote a clause number
  from memory: read the live Terms.
- **Payment holds:** payments can be held while identity, address, tax,
  or policy checks are unresolved. A hold is not the same as a
  forfeiture, and the account's Payments section shows which checks
  are open.
- **Termination and final payment:** the Help Center describes a
  final-payment path for the portion of earnings not identified as
  invalid, after a hold period, in some termination cases. Whether it
  applies depends on the termination reason and the Terms version.
  Treat any claim about a specific case as unproven until the account
  notice and the live Terms are read together.
- **Appeals:** where an appeal route exists, it has a deadline and a
  limit on attempts. Read the notice in the account for the exact
  deadline. Do not submit repeated appeals with no new information.
- **Confidentiality of account data:** do not share publisher IDs,
  payment profile IDs, bank details, or identity documents in public
  places, screenshots, or support threads beyond what Google asks for
  through its own secure channels.

## Revenue share history

- **2003 to early 2024:** publishers received 68% of revenue for
  AdSense for Content display ads and 51% for AdSense for Search,
  within a single transaction.
- **Early 2024 to today:** for AdSense for Content, publishers receive
  80% of revenue after the advertiser platform takes its fee. When the
  buyer is Google Ads, with an average buy-side fee of about 15%, the
  overall publisher share is expected to remain around 68%. AdSense for
  Search stays at 51%. Payment is on a per-impression (eCPM) basis for
  AdSense for Content. See `01-history-timeline.md`.

## How payment works

1. Earnings accrue in the AdSense account during the month.
2. Earnings are finalised early in the following month, after invalid
   traffic deductions.
3. If the balance reaches the payment threshold (US $100 or the local
   equivalent) and all verification holds are clear, payment is
   typically issued between the 21st and 26th of the month.
4. Payment is made by the method set up in the account, such as wire
   transfer, where supported in the payment country.
5. Tax information must be on file where Google requests it. US tax
   withholding may apply to earnings from US viewers for non-US
   publishers without the correct tax form. Check the live tax pages.

Confirm the threshold, timing, and methods on the live Help pages for
the publisher's country. Do not rely on a remembered figure.

## Verification steps and deadlines

Deadlines are shown inside the account and control over anything
written here. The usual sequence is:

1. **Identity verification:** Google asks for an identity document
   when a verification threshold is reached. Submit clear documents
   whose name and date of birth match the payments profile exactly.
   Repeated failed submissions can exhaust attempts and lock the
   front-end flow, so get the first submission right.
2. **Address verification (PIN):** after the account reaches the
   verification earnings level, Google mails a PIN by post. Enter it
   inside the deadline shown in the account. Keep the postal address
   deliverable.
3. **Bank verification:** confirm the bank account details where
   Google asks for a test deposit or document check.
4. **Tax verification:** submit the requested tax form and keep it
   current.

Name mismatches are the most common verification failure: a shortened
name, a different spelling, or a name that does not match the identity
document. Fix the payments profile name to match the legal document
before submitting, where the account allows it, and use one primary
document consistently.

## Holds, suspensions, and terminations: keep them separate

- **Hold:** payment is paused while a check is open. Fix the check and
  the hold can clear.
- **Ad serving disabled or restricted:** ads stop on a page, a site,
  or the account while a policy issue is open. Fix the issue and
  request review where offered.
- **Suspension:** the account is temporarily unable to serve ads or
  receive payment. Follow the notice instructions exactly.
- **Termination / closure:** the account is closed. The notice states
  the reason and any appeal route and deadline. Balance treatment
  depends on the reason and the Terms version. Do not promise a
  payment outcome.

Never merge two accounts' cases. Ticket numbers, balances, names, and
verification states belong to one account only. When helping with a
support case, read the whole thread for that ticket before drafting
anything.
